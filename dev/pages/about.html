About gePARD

gePARD is an interactive online resource for exploring antibiotic resistance-associated sequences identified in tailed bacteriophage genomes.

What is gePARD?

gePARD is a database and visualization resource designed to make phage resistome data easier to explore. It provides information about complete bacteriophage genomes, their hosts, genome characteristics, and resistance-associated sequence analysis.

The database contains information from 5,147 complete genomes of tailed bacteriophages and allows users to browse individual genomes and examine their analysis results.

About the Project

gePARD accompanies research investigating the presence of antibiotic resistance-associated sequences in tailed bacteriophages. The project explores whether phage genomes contain sequences with similarity to known antibiotic resistance genes and examines these findings using both nucleotide- and protein-level analyses.

The goal of gePARD is to provide an accessible way to explore these results and support further investigation of phage genomes and their potential relationship with antibiotic resistance.

Database Contents

5,147

Complete bacteriophage genomes included in the database.

Genome Data

Information including genome length, GC content, host, accession number, and gene count.

Resistance Analysis

Nucleotide- and protein-level resistance-associated sequence hits identified during the analysis.

Research Workflow

The analysis used complete phage genome sequences and compared them with antibiotic resistance-associated sequences. Candidate matches were then filtered using sequence identity and coverage criteria for further investigation.

More information about the data processing and analysis is available on the Methods page.

About the Developer

gePARD was developed as part of a research project investigating resistomes in tailed bacteriophages. The website was created to provide an interactive interface for exploring the resulting genomic data and analysis.
